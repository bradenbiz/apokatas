# Roadmap and delivery lessons

Recorded 2026-10-04. **Status: assistant-synthesized lessons and candidate practices, not accepted requirements.** They come from an assistant-produced study, dated 2026-09-14, of one existing metal-backed money platform's public roadmap statements from 2018 to 2026, and from the user's own observations. The platform is deliberately not named. The user decides which practices become Apokatas commitments.

## What the study observed

The study catalogued 157 announced plans and 592 dated statements about them.

- **Early dates slipped.** 14 of 15 comparable completed plans missed their earliest stated target. Measured from that first target, the median delay was 297 days and the mean about 548. Measured from the latest revised target, the median was 27 days, so estimates improved as delivery approached.
- **Plans without dates drifted.** Plans announced with no date took a median of about 2.5 years to complete.
- **Many plans were never confirmed as delivered.** 61 plans were reported delivered and 16 partly delivered. For 74, delivery could not be established. That is not proof they failed: often the record simply goes quiet.
- **Some launches did not last.** A card programme launched and was later switched off when its providers withdrew from digital assets. Later regional betas did not fulfil the original global plan.
- **Replacements were sometimes presented as continuity.** A small number of plans were explicitly replaced by a different approach. Delivering the replacement is not the same as keeping the original promise.
- **Trials ended.** A more frequent audit schedule was trialled and then dropped back to the old schedule. An ended trial differs from a broken permanent commitment, but only if it was described as a trial.
- **Scope kept widening.** New product lines and yield programmes were announced while basic funding, withdrawal and card functions were still incomplete. The user regards that breadth, coordinated by one organization, as a likely cause of the delays. The study does not establish causation.

**Limits:**
- The timing sample is small, and three linked yield plans share one forecast.
- Statements were attributed to speakers from context, not verified from audio.
- A plan with no confirmed delivery is not necessarily a failure.
- None of this predicts the platform's survival.
- A survey of which delivered features remain available was withdrawn because its checks were selective and uneven.

## Things to avoid

- Announcing dates for work that depends on banks, card issuers, regulators or other partners outside the project's control, without saying so.
- Plans with no date, kept on the roadmap indefinitely with no status.
- Letting a plan go silent instead of marking it paused, cancelled or replaced.
- Describing a replacement as if the original plan had been delivered.
- Counting a launch as permanent when it depends on a partner who can withdraw.
- Starting new product lines or yield programmes while the core user journey is unreliable.
- Using marketing to promote capabilities customers cannot yet use.
- Treating software uptime as proof that money can actually move.

## Candidate practices for Apokatas

These are proposals, not decisions.

1. **A public, dated roadmap with revision history.** Every change of target is recorded with its date and reason, and nothing is silently rewritten.
2. **Explicit stages.** Label each item as idea, plan, estimate, pilot, generally available, paused, withdrawn or replaced. Include geography and eligibility.
3. **Show dependencies.** Name the outside party each item depends on, and how much the team controls the timeline.
4. **Ranges, not single dates,** for anything depending on partners, together with what the estimate is based on.
5. **Track availability, not just launch.** Report whether a delivered capability is still available, where, and for whom.
6. **The core journey before new scope.** Funding, holding, transferring, withdrawing and redeeming must meet published reliability targets before new verticals or incentives are announced. See [operational lessons](operational-lessons.md) and [incentive design lessons](incentive-design-lessons.md).
7. **Define a minimum viable product by what an ordinary user can rely on.** The user's earlier view was that this means essential capabilities a typical customer can understand and depend on, not a flawless or exhaustive product. Their benchmark for a usable metal-money service covered:
   - spot trading
   - easy fiat funding and withdrawals
   - practical physical redemption
   - trustworthy metal and fiat audits
   - affordable storage
   - cards
   - usable interfaces
   - support
   - tax reporting

   This was a benchmark for an existing service, not an Apokatas commitment.
8. **Marketing follows working capability.** Stronger comparative marketing waits until the core works, a view the user expressed before Apokatas existed.
9. **Reuse other businesses' capabilities.** Cooperation between providers, and white-label routes where other businesses can offer spendable, redeemable metal without rebuilding everything, can reduce how much one organization must deliver. This is consistent with the user's [preference for replaceable providers](../rationale.md).

## Comparing services

Useful dimensions when evaluating any metal-money service:
- ownership and the customer's legal claim
- total fees and minimums
- custody and verification
- geographic eligibility
- transferability
- practical liquidity
- service reliability
- account structures
- the difference between an announcement and a usable capability

A token being available, or a high uptime figure, does not show that a usable withdrawal path exists. Scores from LLM-assisted comparisons should not be treated as verified findings.
